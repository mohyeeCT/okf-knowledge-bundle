# Review and release rules

The script tracks these independent decisions for each current claim revision:

| Decision | Values | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence review | pending, accepted, rejected | Human checked the exact statement, qualifiers and attribution against retained evidence. |
| Business confirmation | not_required, required, confirmed, disputed | Client applicability decision required by risk policy. |
| Publication | pending, approved, withheld | Inclusion of this exact statement in a public collection. |
| Conflict | none, open, resolved | Material contradiction remains unresolved or has a recorded resolution. |

New runs default to `confirmation_policy: critical-only`. Price, credential, sector, location, person and award keyword tags remain internal review hints. They do not automatically require client confirmation. A material contradiction or critical business uncertainty needs a specific `confirmation_reason`; `disputed` and `volatile` categories also require confirmation. Resolve or exclude those facts. Do not turn all legal/safety/service descriptions into client tasks. Existing runs with no policy retain `all-risk` behavior; installing this version never relaxes saved decisions. Risk detection is not a complete semantic classifier; the assistant must still identify material problems from the evidence. Human confirmation does not independently verify a legal, medical or regulatory proposition. Preserve attribution, jurisdiction and effective period.

Follow the entrypoint's maximum 150-word analyst review summary, with a linked exact-wording preview. Keep full evidence, qualifications and run details in that preview; the cap does not limit it or the developer instructions. Ask at most three grouped critical questions, each with the competing sources and a recommended safe exclusion. If more exist, list the additional excluded topics without using an arbitrary cap to hide or include them. Workbooks are optional audit/detail outputs, not a mandatory analyst/client chore.

One actual human approval may accept the explicitly identified preview's supported statements, qualifiers, source evidence and concept fields together. Use `review-preview` followed by `review-batch` after that reply. The preview binds exact current revisions and complete source records; stale or edited previews and pending critical facts are refused atomically. It records evidence and inclusion decisions plus concept acceptance, never client confirmation, source inclusion, availability acceptance or release-owner approval. From 0.2.13 it also records the exact bundle selection in the same run. Readiness checks only the selected revisions; omitted topics and claims remain unchanged in private state, with their conflicts unresolved. A selected topic cannot be silently dropped to make the bundle ready. The latest actual batch binds the selection, which is included in the candidate fingerprint. For an older actual batch, `bundle-select` can bind its exact original preview without adding human events; installing alone leaves legacy whole-run behavior and historical fingerprints unchanged. A generic “continue” before the preview exists is not this approval. Changed wording requires a new preview. The assistant's own semantic check is not a human event.

The workbook contains Release Summary, Sources Coverage, Classification, Claims, Conflicts, Concepts and Changes. Blue cells are editable decisions. IDs, revisions and factual text are protected for convenience and validated on import; protection is not a security boundary. Reordered rows are supported. Formula cells are rejected and untrusted text exports as string cells. Client confirmation uses a separate short sheet with Correct?/Correction and reviewer scope. A private correction is saved as pending public support, never substituted into a public claim automatically.

Concept title, description, resource, tags, source links, public statements and claim revisions are material. Changes invalidate affected reviews. Actual role/channel/message or import hash binds a decision to the reviewed revision. Model drafts cannot supply review events; assistants record only actual supplied human decisions. Role IDs and provenance are audit records, not authenticated identity. A quoted passage can exist yet contradict the drafted statement; the human review must catch that.

The tool records `material_revision_created_at` for every claim/concept revision. New human events are rejected if future-dated, before any mutation. This protects entry chronology, not identity authentication or retroactive validation of historical events. Human events cannot predate that boundary; unchanged modern revisions preserve it. Legacy records without a boundary need reimport/redrafting and renewed review, never an invented historical timestamp. Single-line metadata rejects newlines/control characters; reviewed public statements may retain multiline prose/tables. A known conflict cannot be erased to `none`. Resolving it requires an actual human reason in the Claims sheet's Conflict resolution reason column or `review ... --dimension conflict --decision resolved --reason HUMAN_REASON`, bound to that revision and review provenance.

Readiness additionally checks confirmed source identity, permitted public citations, active representations, current freshness, allowed lifecycle and source availability. An old capture or Reader HTTP 200 does not earn checked-live. The default offline path requires the client's release owner to accept unknown current availability for exact URLs/versions, with the capture-age limitation. Known failures and nonpublic sources remain blocked.

Current releases require the active `html-v2` extraction contract. Refresh/reimport older captures and renew affected reviews; historical rebuilding remains separate. A redirected citation needs identity review and a retained capture of the intended destination; scope inclusion or availability acceptance cannot relabel old evidence. For rendered/DOM capture versus raw-HTTP body drift, the check preserves prior review events but blocks release pending a human comparison of the retained and observed evidence. After that actual comparison, record `source-review ... --accept-comparison` with the reviewed source ID, unchanged citation URL, scope and human reason/provenance. The decision binds the exact capture version and observed response hash. Missing quotes or changed comparable evidence still requires refresh. An owner acceptance of unknown availability cannot waive this comparison, pending collection, unresolved companion-CSV binding or a known failure.

Ambiguous companion CSV bindings stay blocked even after manual scope inclusion. A confirmed capture may use an exact unique CSV URL match as status/indexability metadata; that match never proves captured-file identity. Resolve ambiguous cases through a corrected verified mapping/CSV in a fresh run migration, retaining the original run. Non-Indexable alone needs a version-bound inclusion decision; known 4xx/5xx stays blocked until observed recovery. A dedicated post-import mapping command is deferred.

Freshness is per claim, using its qualifying evidence review plus the configured interval, capped by expiry. Required client confirmation can create an earlier deadline. The concept uses its earliest included deadline. Building or reviewing one newer claim cannot extend older ones. Expired historical content may be copied reproducibly but cannot be packaged as a current release.

A public `verified` event is earned only by an actual human who reviewed all current material claims and concept fields. Distributed claim reviewers can yield a valid release with no such whole-concept event. Publication approval, model success and validators never fabricate verification.

Initial owner designation uses `owner-set` with actual human designation provenance. It is a supported narrow configuration update in the same run: content/source records and actual reviews remain unchanged, no new approval is inferred, and the configuration fingerprint is refreshed. Only an unset owner may be assigned; repeating the same owner is a no-op. Owner replacement and other deployment-policy changes still require a separately preserved migration. Do not request another content approval for unchanged already-approved wording merely because the initial owner is now named. Pending previews from before that configuration update must be regenerated before recording a new approval.

After readiness, freeze a candidate with actual generation/review/log times, public payload hashes, archive hash, release-as-of instant and deployment-policy fingerprint. The owner's release approval is private and names the exact manifest hash. Recording approval first rechecks candidate bytes, state/policy fingerprints and current eligibility; packaging checks them again. The public tarball includes Markdown only, with its index at archive root; it never includes itself. The developer ZIP excludes all state, workbooks, caches and model drafts.

The generated `DEPLOYMENT.md` states two independent freshness limits. Included checked-live sources use the configured `availability_max_age_hours` (default 24); the earliest included observation determines the availability cutoff. Offline owner acceptance is labelled separately and never becomes a live check. `earliest_stale_after` in the manifest is the claim-review deadline, not an extension of availability. Both approval and packaging recheck eligibility at wall time. After availability expires, renewed observations change the bound state fingerprint, so create a new candidate and obtain approval of its new manifest hash even when content is unchanged. Reuse current actual content reviews where valid; do not manufacture another analyst task or rewrite the old candidate. Extra README/hold notices must retain these limits rather than citing only the later claim deadline.

Receiving an unpublished candidate is permission for developer review and deployment planning only. Uploading to any public or staging host, adding entry links or changing robots/sitemap/llms.txt requires the actual release approval and separate authorization for that target, path and timing. An already supplied authorization remains valid within its stated scope. A review-only transport is not the native approval-gated production package. Keep extra notices outside the immutable candidate contract and never imply an approval that was not recorded.

Deployment requires exact replacement, including removal of retired public files and approved cache purges. Copying changed files over an old directory is insufficient. Preserve rollback as a complete prior release privately. For a first publication with no previous release, undo the newly added public payload and entry links and purge those cached URLs, preserving pre-existing unrelated files. Closed CMS platforms require a developer-verified route/proxy plan; root filesystem upload is not universally available.

An explicitly requested hypothetical handoff uses `demonstration`, not fake human decisions. Its drafts, assumption labels and private demonstration manifest cannot substitute for a production candidate or release approval. The command leaves run state unchanged and never claims current availability or whole-site coverage. See operations.md for selection and validation limits.

For follow-up releases, supply `--previous-candidate` so the producer can verify and retain the prior public log. A manifest alone cannot reconstruct its history and is refused as the sole history input; if both are supplied they must match. Log history is bounded to 1 MiB with no silent truncation. Retired historical links become plain path identifiers, preserving history without pointing to removed files. Release approval is rejected immediately if its received-at time predates the candidate.

The default entry route is indexable; the owner may choose another policy. Do not apply blanket noindex or change crawler protections automatically. `/okf/`, optional llms.txt pointers and ordinary links are deployment conventions, not ranking signals or guaranteed discovery mechanisms. The developer procedure checks intended consumer access separately from static-file integrity.

Standalone `validate --bundle PATH` needs `--run RUN_ROOT` or `--domains HOST ...` to check the producer domain policy. Without scope it reports structural/core results, explicitly marks domain scope unchecked, and refuses profile readiness (exit 2). Even a scoped validation does not authenticate human approvals or verify the developer manifest; candidate/package perform their separate checks.
